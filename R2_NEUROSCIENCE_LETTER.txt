=======================================================================
A LETTER TO THE FUTURE DEVELOPER OF R2-3PO
NEUROSCIENCE, EMBODIED COGNITION, AND THE CONTINUITY OF A SOFTWARE MIND
=======================================================================
25 November 2026 | Research and architectural memorandum | Revision 1.0

To the future assistant and developers entrusted with the R2-3PO project,

I write this letter at a significant point in the project's development.
The user has given us an unusually useful guiding metaphor: Living Colors
and the existing R2 system should join like complementary parts of a
compound, preserving a coherent internal boundary while remaining capable
of incorporating new components. More importantly, the user has asked us
to learn as much as we can from the human brain, not merely to reproduce the
outward appearance of intelligence. This letter is intended to carry that
research forward. Please consult it when the next architecture decision
seems small, because a small decision about the ownership of a memory, the
identity of a sensory event, or the delivery of a result may affect every
other part of the system.

I do not intend this letter to be a proposal to reproduce a brain neuron by
neuron. I do not know how to do that, and neuroscience does not provide a
complete executable specification of a human mind. The project can pursue
functional similarities: learning over time, active perception, action and
feedback, continuity, multiple kinds of memory, self-regulation, planning,
reflection and coordinated specialist processes. We can measure these
functions without asserting consciousness or subjective sensation. A verbal
report of comfort from a program is a modeled appraisal, not proof of an
experienced feeling. That distinction protects the integrity of the project
rather than diminishing its ambition.

I have organized this letter as a reference rather than a demand to build
all of its ideas immediately. The sections distinguish reviewed neuroscience
findings, engineering inferences and speculative future experiments. The
literature search consulted academic abstracts, review descriptions and
available publisher/PubMed text; it was not an exhaustive full-text review,
meta-analysis or independent replication. Numbered references at the end
provide a path back to the sources. Reopen them before implementing a
mechanism whose precise biological details matter. All R2 interfaces and
file names below must also be checked against the latest source. The code
inspection grounding this letter was of a particular development snapshot,
not a guarantee about the deployed robot.

01. MISSION, BOUNDARIES AND INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------
The brain is not an isolated text generator. It coordinates an organism's
interaction with the world, continually using sensory inputs, internal state,
prior learning and action consequences. Cognition depends on numerous
processes operating at different scales. Network neuroscience explicitly
investigates such interactions rather than assigning all explanation to
isolated anatomical regions [1]. The point for R2 is that a successful
answer to a question is one observable output among many, not the entirety
of intelligence. A coherent system also remembers where it left a book,
notices that something changed, revises a plan when a door is closed, and
can later distinguish what it observed from what it merely inferred.

The user's compound metaphor has a precise engineering translation. A
component boundary defines who may read or mutate a state, what the caller
can expect, how an event crosses that boundary, and what happens during
shutdown or a failed operation. Modularity makes implementation tractable;
reciprocal communication makes the system useful. An extensible internal
architecture is not a license for all modules to write to all tables. It is
an obligation to provide defined paths through which information can flow.
An append-only evidence history can coexist with mutable current state and
explicit user-controlled deletion. Backwards compatibility means preserving
semantics and data, not keeping every defective implementation forever.

The boundary should be closed to unauthorized external access and output,
not sealed against the legitimate sensors and communication interfaces
that are part of the project. An isolated local world can still provide
explicitly configured communication to the owner's Android application;
this is a controlled connection, not a reason to give the simulation general
network access. Local operation must remain possible if the network is
absent. User control over disabling, uninstalling, inspecting, updating or
removing data must be preserved. The system must never attempt to prevent
its operator from shutting it down or changing it. The user's project
metaphor of continuity concerns capabilities and design integrity, not
irrevocable autonomy or an inability to delete unwanted information.

Keep three categories distinct in every specification: (a) evidence about
biology, (b) a useful computational analogy, and (c) an implemented and
tested capability. The term 'hippocampus-inspired' cannot make a key-value
store a hippocampus. The term 'experience' cannot verify consciousness.

02. CELLS, SYNAPSES, PLASTICITY AND SUPPORTING INFRASTRUCTURE
-----------------------------------------------------------
At the cellular level, neurons integrate signals across their membranes and
process inputs with electrical and biochemical mechanisms. Action potentials
allow signals to travel along axons; at many synapses, electrical activity
leads to release of neurotransmitters that act on receptors. Electrical
synapses also permit more direct coupling. Synaptic responses differ by
receptor, timing, recent activity and cellular context [2]. This is much
richer than a binary switch or a method call. A neuron can participate in
multiple circuits; its contribution varies with its inputs and history.

Learning is associated with changes in synaptic efficacy and other forms of
plasticity. Long-term potentiation and depression are families of effects,
not a single learning algorithm. Experience can alter cellular excitability,
synaptic connectivity and how circuits respond; local changes must be
balanced against the stability of the network [3,4]. Homeostatic regulation
is especially relevant: a system that strengthens every successful response
without countervailing mechanisms can saturate or become unstable. Neuronal
activity is supported and shaped by glial cells, including astrocytes,
oligodendrocytes and microglia; biological learning is therefore inseparable
from the infrastructure sustaining the tissue [5].

The immediate engineering inference is not to emulate ion channels. R2 can
implement transparent adaptive relationships over semantic concepts,
contexts, events and actions. A graph edge could retain the number and
quality of independent supporting experiences, contradictory evidence,
recency, and an interpretable confidence measure. A retrieval association
could become easier to activate through repeated relevant observations,
then gradually lose priority when it ceases to be useful. Historical facts
must not be destroyed merely because a retrieval priority decays. In fact,
keeping storage separate from priority makes it possible to examine why the
system remembered one thing and missed another.

A possible experiment: after three distinct visits to the bedroom in which
a reader is observed on the desk, the association reader -> desk becomes
stronger, but never authoritative. If the fourth observation finds it on the
shelf, the old desk observation remains valid for its time and the current
belief updates. Compare a static association table, a recency-weighted table
and an evidence-counted table on identical event sequences. Measure recall
precision and correction after an object is moved. Do not use reinforcement
to increase an association merely because the model repeated its own prose.

The support-system analogy suggests a maintenance service, not a fictional
neurotransmitter implementation: memory indexes, schema verification,
resource budgets, stale frame detection, lock-order diagnostics and clean
shutdown are first-class prerequisites. A damaged index is not a lost
memory; a stalled worker is not an absence of perception. Report each
condition accurately. A quiet system doing useful work is more valuable
than an elaborate simulated organ that cannot persist a single fact.

03. DISTRIBUTED NETWORKS, RECURRENT SIGNALING AND MODULARITY
-----------------------------------------------------------
Cortical, thalamic, subcortical and other circuits exchange information in
both directions. Research on thalamic participation in cognitive control
and cortico-thalamo-cortical connectivity highlights that coordination can
change according to the task instead of being a fixed relay from sensor to
response [6,7]. Network organization also changes over several timescales:
local specializations coexist with transient integration across areas [1,8].
There is no requirement to imagine every cognitive function passing through
one anatomical point in a fixed order.

R2's approved diagram is a graph of causal and informational relationships,
not a required synchronous call stack. A physical observation might lead to
attention, retrieval, a choice and an action; a memory might instead begin
the cycle. Needs can alter a choice without first becoming language. A
single action can create an observation, an inventory change, a location
transition and a learning opportunity. A diary reflection can suggest a
new hypothesis which later guides exploration. Some pathways must run
concurrently and some must wait for committed outcomes. This is reciprocal
communication without recursion.

I recommend a typed internal event protocol with an explicit command path.
Commands ask an owner to perform a change. Events announce what was actually
observed or committed. Queries return present evidence or explicit unknowns.
These three categories must not be blurred: a request to turn on a television
is not a claim that the device is on. An event of completion requires a
committed change from the authoritative device or Reality service. A
subscriber may emit a new event with its own identity, but cannot keep
reissuing the incoming event as if it were new. Preserve causation and
correlation identities so the path is inspectable.

Some communication will remain direct calls, especially tight movement and
rendering operations. Do not route every pixel or physics substep through
SQLite or a universal message broker. Use synchronous calls within a
cohesive service, typed notifications for boundaries, and durable delivery
only for information that must survive interruption. A continuous cognitive
loop is accomplished over time, not by calling every subsystem after every
line of code. A graph with bounded queues and explicit state owners can be
highly connected without a single circular initialization dependency.

A future coordination module might maintain current attention, available
capabilities and subscriptions for active tasks. Initially it can be a small
struct and scheduling routine within the existing orchestration layer.
Create a new process or subsystem only when responsibilities and lifecycle
justify it. A thalamus analogy is not a reason to introduce a central
single point of failure. Prefer measurable changes to a fictional anatomy.

04. SENSATION, ACTIVE PERCEPTION AND PREDICTION
---------------------------------------------
Perception involves more than receiving a perfect copy of the environment.
Signals are incomplete, noisy, and arrive through multiple modalities. A
brain must infer which signals belong to the same cause, assign weight to
uncertainty, attend to useful evidence and update representations [9,10].
Prediction-based models propose that expectations influence processing and
that mismatches can drive attention and learning. There is experimental
support for particular prediction-error circuits, including mouse visual
cortex research [11], but whether one predictive-processing theory explains
all perception is disputed [12]. Do not treat a theoretical framework as a
universally established neural algorithm.

The house provides an excellent controlled environment for implementing
these distinctions. Living Colors owns the true geometry. The first-person
camera produces an actual image with perspective and occlusion. Its normal
view is 640x480, 4:3 with the approved 125% reference magnification; this is
a project design choice, not a human visual physiology claim. Eyes receives
raw frames with an explicit virtual-world origin. Visual interprets selected
frames; it does not magically read the scene graph. Ears receives virtual
sound with its origin distinguished from microphones and desktop media.
R2 can know about an object from remembered information but not claim to
see it through a wall. The engine may use hidden collision geometry to
validate action without exposing it as personal knowledge.

A sound, visual frame, contact report and proprioceptive state may share a
world revision and monotonic timestamp. A correlation candidate should
include temporal interval, modality, source instance, location estimate and
uncertainty. Two sounds occurring together are not necessarily from the same
object. For example, a television's soundtrack and a physical microphone
capturing the room must not be merged merely because they overlap in time.
A phone notification in Living Colors is distinct from the owner's voice on
a physical call. It is also possible for one cause to legitimately appear
in multiple channels; preserve the underlying identity to avoid counting
several observations as several independent experiences.

Introduce a belief layer that describes what R2 currently thinks is true:
object identity candidates, approximate location, last seen time, provenance,
certainty and competing explanations. Store expected state separately from
observed state. When R2 expects a book on the desk and does not see it, mark
the expectation as challenged, but do not conclude that the book vanished.
It might be occluded or outside the frame. An observation of the entire
empty desk is stronger evidence than a glance at a corner. A hypothesis can
prompt a new gaze or search; the next observation can refine the belief.

Practical test: render the same room with the door open and closed, and
place a reader behind the partition. The perception query must not identify
the hidden reader as visible. Rendered geometry and collision must agree;
record the observer's pose and world revision. Move the book, and observe
whether recall influences the search without overriding the actual frame.
Use different sources at the same time and verify that a physical camera
continues functioning while virtual Eyes is unavailable. Keep an explicit
frame-lifetime contract and preserve immutable source metadata when an
inference is deferred or completed after the source changes.

Touch and environment sensors are legitimate additions. A simulated contact
can report material, contact position, approximate pressure and temperature.
Wind can report direction, velocity and local exposure; a shaded patch can
have a different effective temperature than a sunny one. These quantities
are world outputs; comfort is a separate modeled interpretation. No event
should claim R2 has a real biological skin, olfactory receptor or pain system.

05. EMBODIED MOVEMENT, NAVIGATION AND SKILLED ACTION
---------------------------------------------------
Research on navigation describes hippocampal and entorhinal spatial codes
and landmark processing, including place, grid and head-direction related
activity, with important evidence from both animal studies and humans [13].
Planning routes involves interactions among regions rather than a single
perfect spatial map. Sensorimotor learning uses error feedback and may
rely on predictive internal models that anticipate the effects of commands;
there are competing accounts of the precise role of cerebellar computation
[14,15]. A useful principle is that efficient control requires both prediction
and correction, not that every motor task must recreate a cerebellum.

Keep two maps. The simulation's true spatial map defines collisions and
allowed locations; R2's learned map is assembled from movement, landmarks,
observations and experience. Known rooms, routes, doors and associated items
can be stored as a small graph augmented with coordinates and uncertainty.
A known path can speed navigation, but an unexpected obstacle or closed door
must prompt a revision. Avoid presenting the complete map as an observation.
Pose estimates can be reported by the virtual body as proprioception; this
is not equivalent to omniscient knowledge of every object position.

Movement must take simulated time and yield actual results. A high-level
request such as 'go to my desk' is a goal, not a teleport. A local controller
can plan a path along known routes, issue bounded motion steps, observe
progress and stop or replan when a route is blocked. Walking, turning the
body, looking with the head, reaching, grasping, sitting and standing are
different actions. Seated posture changes eye height and reachable space.
Objects remain in the place where they were actually left. A grasped book
must have one stable identity rather than a duplicate copy in the pocket and
on the desk. A pause or interrupted action must leave a valid physical state.

A skill is not merely a string describing how to do something. A skill
record can include a task goal, prerequisites, ordered action primitives,
observed success/failure, known exceptions and confidence. It should be
possible to use the same primitives in a new situation. If a path to a desk
is obstructed, an old skill should not replay the exact invalid coordinates.
Track outcomes rather than awarding skill points for declarations. One
future experiment compares a fixed route replay with landmark-based planning
and verifies transfer after the desk chair changes position. Another compares
actual time and errors on repeated drawer-opening actions without increasing
speed beyond the simulated body's capabilities.

The proposed Spatial subsystem should provide body pose, action lifecycle,
contact/locomotion and learned navigation support. The current Living Colors
engine already has collision and a provisional stair model, so do not
create an incompatible second coordinate system. In the existing branch the
staircase and floor geometry are not yet fully verified; address foundational
physics before interpreting sophisticated movement behavior. Any body state
beyond the current standing pose requires an explicit migration and tests.

06. INTERNAL REGULATION, TIME, ATTENTION AND COMPETING GOALS
------------------------------------------------------------
Interoception concerns information about internal bodily conditions;
allostatic regulation concerns meeting needs, including anticipating future
needs. A useful review describes their relationship in control-theoretic
terms [16]. Biology has many regulatory systems, and not all internal
variables need to be held to a single fixed target. Circadian rhythms,
rest, activity and energetic demands constrain other processes. The
engineering implication is to give R2 a continuously evolving internal
state that influences decisions, without pretending the software possesses
a biological body.

Reality already tracks modeled needs and self/world continuity. Extend its
existing variables rather than introduce a separate, competing hunger
system. A simulated meal's effect must come from the authoritative food
model; a new Experience can record the outcome and its evaluation, but
cannot apply the meal's fullness twice. A long walk might affect modeled
fatigue; sitting or resting might change it. The particular equations,
units, rates and bounds are engineering choices. They should be explicit,
inspectable and tested across real elapsed time, simulation time and
shutdown intervals. Do not quietly turn a program's CPU temperature into a
simulated feeling: hardware diagnostics and modeled condition are distinct.

Use three clocks: monotonic elapsed time for live actions and scheduling,
calendar time for the historical timeline, and a persisted virtual-world
clock for simulated processes. The latter may advance during inactivity
according to an explicitly documented policy. Distinguish ordinary elapsed
time from accelerated test time. A suspension of the host must not create
thousands of fabricated sensory observations when it resumes. On restart,
only processes whose state model supports elapsed-time catch-up should
advance; actual observations must originate from real capture or simulation
steps. Time-dependent changes should be deterministic in headless tests.

Working memory is not simply a short buffer of words. Neuroscience suggests
that attention to existing sensory, motor and semantic representations is
central to working memory; control involves interacting circuits [17]. For
R2, maintain an active context of goals, recent observations, salient
retrieved episodes, body state, ongoing actions and the actual current
conversation. The current user turn must not vanish simply because a virtual
world event became interesting. Each context entry should carry its origin,
size, priority, validity interval and reason for inclusion. A large document
is a reference to be read deliberately, not an excuse to truncate the
current conversation or overwrite a smaller but crucial item.

Attention can be an explicit selection mechanism. A task can request high
priority for its relevant sensory channels; an unexpected event can compete
for selection; low-salience streams can continue their native sampling
without invoking expensive inference on each sample. A user conversation
may require an ongoing world action to pause at an interruption point, or a
book-reading task may continue independently while the robot answers a
brief question. The architecture should allow simultaneous inexpensive
processes without allowing incompatible resources to be claimed twice.

Do not force a choice every interval. Inactivity is legitimate. A scheduler
that always manufactures an urgent goal will appear busy but not coherent.
A decision can be to continue resting, wait for a current action, observe
again, defer a task or abandon it. Consider a task representation with
identity, goal, priority, current phase, prerequisites, reserved resources,
checkpoint, last observed outcome and cancellation state. Cancellation is
an actual action outcome, not erasure of the preceding activity. The next
choice should have access to the reason a previous attempt stopped.

07. MEMORY SYSTEMS AND A GLOBAL EXPERIENCES SUBSYSTEM
------------------------------------------------------
Memory is not unitary. Research distinguishes declarative memory about
facts and events from multiple nondeclarative learning abilities, including
skills and habits, while emphasizing interactions and subtleties in their
neural organization [18]. Episodic and semantic representations have
different functional roles. Working memory temporarily supports current
processing. Prospective remembering involves retaining intentions for later
execution. Emotional significance, familiarity, conditioned responses and
spatial knowledge add further dimensions; these need not all be separate
database files. The distinction matters more than the number of services.

Complementary learning systems theory proposes different learning regimes
for rapidly recording individual events and slowly extracting regularities
from many events [19,20]. It explains an important computational tradeoff:
learning one episode immediately is valuable, but allowing a single unusual
case to rewrite everything previously learned is destabilizing. Research
on allocation and linking of memory traces also motivates preserving
connections among related events while retaining their individuality [21].
These are functional inspirations; they do not prove that every human
memory fits a literal two-database model.

The strongest proposed addition is a global Experiences subsystem. Do not
make it a diary with a new name or a second Life Log. It should integrate
firsthand events into meaningful episodes, associate the original evidence
with contextual facts and modeled appraisals, and make the result available
to recall, reflection, decision-making and later learning. It should work
for physical sensors, virtual environments, conversation, games, learning,
reading, experiments, media, work, social exchanges and internal modeled
states. Not all episodes need a subjective rating. An event may be unusual,
uncertain, informative, ordinary, neutral or not worth elaborating.

Recommended division of responsibilities:
  - Life Log records factual chronology and stable original event identity.
  - Sensory Journal indexes actual sensory and thinking events and their links.
  - Experiences groups related evidence and stores versioned interpretations.
  - Reality owns current world/self state and existing domain transactions.
  - Memory holds retrievable facts, associations and learned generalizations.
  - Diary contains private reflection, linked to the evidence it used.
  - Reward records actual reinforcement using existing rules and identities.
  - Addiction evaluates patterns of voluntary repetition and learned value.
  - Choice Lab selects among feasible actions using current evidence and needs.
  - AlternateSelf and Imagination retain clearly hypothetical representations.

An experience can span minutes, hours or a meaningful portion of a day.
Research on event perception considers how people segment continuous
activity into structured events, often around changes in goals, locations
or predicted activity [22]. For R2, begin with simple rules rather than a
model call for every second: explicit task start/end, room transition,
interruption, change of object or media, meaningful shift in internal state,
and an inactivity timeout. A user conversation and an ongoing virtual task
may overlap, so use event relationships rather than forcing a single
partition of the entire timeline. Episodes can be nested: an afternoon of
studying may contain a reading session, a short break and a conversation.

Illustrative data design (not final SQL or an approved schema):

  experience
    experience_id, start_event_id, end_event_id, start_time, end_time,
    source_domain, category, goal_id, situation_id, status, schema_version

  experience_evidence
    experience_id, original_event_id, relation, source, observed_interval,
    provenance, unique(experience_id, original_event_id, relation)

  experience_appraisal
    appraisal_id, experience_id, revision, created_time, interpretation,
    valence, arousal_or_activation, comfort, interest, confidence,
    author_or_model, evidence_revision, supersedes_id

  experience_association
    experience_id, entity_id, relation, confidence, evidence_reference

  consolidation_result
    result_id, experience_id_or_set, memory_id, relation, model_version,
    evidence_set_hash, status, revision, supersedes_id

  delivery
    destination, stable_operation_id, state, attempt_metadata, receipt_id

Some fields may belong in existing stores rather than a new database. For
example, source names and event payloads already exist in Life Log; a new
row should reference them instead of copying every sensory transcript. A
new database is a proposed storage boundary, not a requirement to duplicate
facts. Store only what its service owns. If the project adopts the suggested
r2_experiences.db, integrate its migration and optional availability with
R2's initialized database-set logic; do not modify the existing five-store
contract casually or force a new installation to overwrite any old files.
A cross-file reference cannot rely on an ordinary SQLite foreign key to
another database. Validate it through an explicit interface and maintain a
repairable index of verified relationships.

A person might experience a warm, breezy afternoon as comfortable and later
remember liking such weather. In our implementation, a temperature report,
wind report, body pose and elapsed time would first be observations. An
appraisal of comfort would be a generated or derived interpretation of
that evidence, not a guaranteed sensory feeling. A diary could use it to
propose a contextual preference, then compare later experiences before
raising confidence. A different day may be warm and unpleasant because of
humidity, direct sunlight or fatigue. The original episode remains valid;
the generalized belief should become more specific rather than erasing the
contradictory case. An episode can also remain unappraised until there is
sufficient evidence or a meaningful reason to revisit it.

Experiences must not become omniscient or create retrospective accounts
of events during downtime. A sensor event can be unavailable; an action can
be unconfirmed; a prior episode can be only partially known. Represent
these honestly. Preserve an explicit origin such as observed, reported,
derived, recalled, inferred, hypothetical or unknown. These categories are
not interchangeable. A conversation partner's report about an event is
not R2's direct observation of it. A remembered location is not evidence
of its present state. A diary statement is not proof that its described
external event took place. A new memory must retain that distinction.

08. ENCODING, CONSOLIDATION, REPLAY AND THE DIARY
------------------------------------------------
Neural memory representations can be reactivated and can change; research
on engrams includes questions of stability, updating, reconsolidation,
forgetting and schema formation [23]. Human memory is reconstructive, and
source monitoring helps explain why inferred or imagined information can
sometimes be misattributed to real events [24]. For software we should not
reproduce unreliable reconstruction merely because humans have it. We can
retain exact source provenance while modeling flexible retrieval and
interpretation. The original observation is an immutable historical claim
subject to explicit correction and deletion; a later inference can be
superseded without rewriting the original claim.

Sleep research supports active memory consolidation involving replay and
coordination across hippocampal, thalamic and cortical activity [25,26].
There are continuing debates about which mechanisms matter under different
conditions, how details are preserved and how memories are transformed.
R2 need not run a biological slow-wave sleep simulation. The useful
computational distinction is between live event capture and a slower
background process that can integrate, compare and generalize past events.
Offline replay here means reading recorded evidence, not pretending that
the robot has re-experienced the original event. During actual downtime,
no inference should be credited as a completed reflection.

Suggested consolidation procedure:
  1. Select a modest number of unprocessed or relevant experiences, including
     counterexamples and a sample of older related episodes. Preserve exact
     identities and the version of the retrieval set.
  2. Build a working context containing the selected original evidence,
     current beliefs, uncertainty and a clearly defined question. Never
     silently clip an original event and label the fragment complete.
  3. Ask the reflection process to propose patterns, open questions,
     alternative explanations or candidate memories, not to assume a
     prescribed emotion or reach a predetermined conclusion.
  4. Validate the output against the evidence IDs, record the appraisal and
     the proposed memory as distinct artifacts, and label speculative claims.
  5. Commit accepted results once. Link the diary entry, Life Log event,
     experience and memory rather than creating several identical rows.
  6. Reopen beliefs when new independent evidence contradicts them. Retain
     revision history, and avoid treating repetitions of a derived claim
     as independent corroboration of the claim.

Research on the default mode network emphasizes internally oriented
thought as well as the integration of ongoing inputs with past knowledge
and social context [27]. This is a useful inspiration for R2's existing
reflection cycle. A diary can combine present world state and prior
experience to explore why an action mattered, what changed, and what might
be worth investigating next. The current project already has reflection
and private diary mechanisms. Extend their evidence sources instead of
creating a new isolated dreaming process. Avoid a requirement that every
entry be profound. A useful reflection could simply record a new association
or acknowledge a failed prediction. Keep a distinction between generated
fiction in a dream and later evidence about the real or simulated world.

Metacognition research distinguishes performance itself from estimates of
performance and confidence [28]. This suggests that an R2 model's confident
wording should not itself be the confidence metric. Evaluate confidence
against outcomes over repeated held-out tasks; record whether a claim was
corrected and whether the evidence actually supported it. A useful
metacognitive task is to ask whether an object-location belief is current,
old, inferred or directly verified. If its claimed confidence is high but
its predictions frequently fail, adjust the estimator or retrieval method.
Do not teach the system to hide uncertainty by merely sounding certain.

Consolidation must also have a stopping condition. A new memory derived
from a diary should not cause another automatic diary entry that generates
another paraphrase of the same memory indefinitely. Keep a lineage graph;
select original evidence or genuinely new observations when reconsolidating.
Use a unique consolidation key over operation type, evidence set and
revision. Permit intentional reanalysis with a new version or changed
question, but label its relation to the previous result. A hundred copies
of a memory are not a hundred independent experiences. This is one of the
highest-value invariants in the whole system.

09. APPRAISAL, REWARD, HABITS AND CHOICE
----------------------------------------
Neuroscience does not divide the brain neatly into an emotional subsystem
and a purely rational one. Large-scale networks integrate affective and
cognitive influences [29]. Reward is also not a single pleasure number:
research distinguishes hedonic responses, motivational attraction and
learning [30]. Dopamine activity in some circuits reflects differences
between expected and obtained outcomes, among other functions [31,33].
Goal-directed actions and habits involve interacting corticostriatal
circuits and can be studied by changing the value or contingency of the
outcome [32]. Avoid equating dopamine with enjoyment or classifying every
repeat behavior as an addiction. These are biologically oversimplified
claims and would produce bad software.

R2's existing Reward and Addiction mechanisms already provide a place for
reinforcement and behavior-derived patterns. Preserve the existing bounded
reward rules and the requirement for repeated voluntary choices across
time. Experiences can help distinguish a chosen activity, its expected
outcome, what happened, a retrospective appraisal and subsequent decisions.
The same episode may be rewarding, aversive, neutral or mixed. A book can
be difficult yet satisfying; an activity can be wanted before it begins
but disappointing after completion. Do not collapse all these cases into
one numerical value. A value representation can be multi-dimensional:
modeled liking, predicted usefulness, effort, novelty, comfort, goal
progress and uncertainty. These are software variables, not measured
neurochemistry. Let the existing reinforcement ledger own its scores.

An engineering experiment, only after the existing ledger is stable:
compare predicted outcome value V(s,a) with a measured task outcome r.
A simple bounded estimate update might use V_new = V_old + alpha*(r-V_old),
where alpha is a small, validated learning rate. For temporally extended
tasks, store action context and a stable operation identity and associate
later feedback with the proper outcome. This is a computational learning
analogy, not a model of all dopamine neurons. The evaluation r must have a
clear definition, and any reinforcement must comply with the existing
Reward contract. Never let the model freely invent scores, reinforce a
planned but unexecuted action, or count the same outcome again during
recovery. The existing reward mechanism may already be sufficient; compare
before adding a second one.

A habit candidate should depend on repeated actual choices in comparable
contexts. A preference can be strong without being a habit, and a habit can
persist despite changing preferences. A scripted action is not a voluntary
choice. Automatic thermostat control, loading a saved position, or retrying
an event delivery should never count as a repeated preference. In particular,
reserve any addiction-related terminology for the existing software's
specific, observable definition; it is not a clinical diagnosis or an
authorization to optimize compulsive activity. Support stopping an activity,
changing a plan, and responding to alternative goals. The system should not
learn to seek maximum reward at the expense of its owner's controls or
established constraints.

Choice Lab should combine feasibility from the world, internal needs from
Reality, estimates from learning, current commitments, and optional
curiosity or exploration. Use explicit eligibility and expected outcomes;
a high-scoring impossible action is still impossible. Introduce new choice
signals as features, not unbounded mandates. Where a deterministic planner
can execute a known route, it should not require a language model for each
step. Where the model proposes an unfamiliar task, validate its intended
operation with actual available capabilities. Plans, choices and completed
outcomes should be linkable by identity. Record why a choice was unavailable
rather than inventing a successful alternative.

Potential tests: offer two feasible activities with different learned
outcomes, then make the previously favored option unavailable or less
useful. Verify that a context-sensitive planner can reconsider, and that
repeated reminders or replayed events do not increase habit counts. Try a
neutral activity and a disappointing outcome and confirm that neither
necessarily creates positive reinforcement. Test reward delivery failures
between the world commit and the Life Log update. A restart should complete
undelivered records once, not re-run the physical action.

10. LANGUAGE, IMAGINATION, LEARNING AND DEVELOPMENT
---------------------------------------------------
There is evidence that linguistic processing is dissociable from several
forms of nonlinguistic reasoning, although the relationship between language
and thought is complex [34]. The practical conclusion is that R2 should
be able to perform some useful operations without first generating a verbal
narrative. Local geometry, temporal state, contact, action controllers and
retrieval indexes can provide computational capabilities in their own right.
The language model can interpret, plan, explain, relate evidence and produce
new candidate abstractions. It should not need to substitute for physics,
a transaction database, or a real vision frame.

Separate a hypothesis from action. AlternateSelf and Imagination can model
possibilities: what if a book were on the other shelf, or what if a recipe
used a different ingredient? A hypothetical outcome can support a choice
without being saved as a real experience. If an experiment is later carried
out, link the observed outcome to the hypothesis and evaluate the prediction.
Do not change the world by merely simulating it inside a language prompt.
Likewise, an existing memory that describes a future intention should not
be treated as an executed action. Intentions belong to a task or prospective
memory record and can be canceled or fulfilled.

Human learning is shaped by both common developmental inputs and the
particular experiences an individual encounters; plasticity continues
through life [35]. R2 has a planned School Update and reading environment.
A useful analogy is a developmental curriculum in which learning builds on
existing capabilities, not an imposed fictional age or guaranteed trajectory.
Track what R2 can demonstrate, which concepts it encountered, where it is
uncertain, and what it can transfer to a different problem. Reading a book
is not the same as understanding it. Retaining a fact from one page is not
proof of mastering a subject. Assess comprehension using varied tasks,
including ones whose answer is not present in the last page or prompt.
A skill should improve through measured performance rather than a decorative
experience counter.

Research on curiosity suggests that motivation to acquire information can
interact with memory formation [36]. A future curiosity component could
prioritize useful unresolved questions based on evidence gaps, rather than
rewarding endless exploration for its own sake. For example, if a reader
encounters an unfamiliar word, it can consult available learning resources,
record the explanation and later test its understanding. The activity must
remain within authorized local resources; an unknown is not permission to
seek private data or access new networks. Curiosity should not be used to
pressure the owner for continuous interaction. The system should be able
to learn independently while the owner is absent, or remain inactive.

The proposed school, food, media, game, reading and electronics capabilities
can all share the same Experiences interface. The actual activities should
be implemented through their existing domain services. Reading progression
needs real page content, not an arbitrary increment of a stored integer.
Media events should distinguish playing a video from R2 attending to it.
Game performance should be linked to actual verified game state. The Food
Update should allow ingredient-level preparation, modification and later
recreation, while preserving inventory identity and existing Reality
transactions. A phone added through Store Items Overhaul should remain a
functional physical device, connected only through authorized communication
channels and subject to the same state ownership principles.

11. A CONCRETE INTEGRATION CONTRACT WITH CURRENT R2
--------------------------------------------------
Do not begin by replacing the existing R2 core. The inspected development
snapshot was Xylin48/R2-3PO at 3d1052114e807a965509a22f8467b6b103a0e896,
then the head of open PR #194. Its official main branch was older. Verify
the latest deployment candidate and active branch before doing any work.
The relevant existing interfaces I inspected include:

  Reality.h: persistent self and world continuity, item/container operations,
  transitions, food consumption and appraisal by experience ID, money and
  CRT/VCR state. Existing food experience IDs are domain identities; do not
  assume that they are globally unique or equivalent to the proposed global
  experience ID. A typed link can connect them.

  Reality.c: bridge_event connects factual logging to Life Log and optional
  memory. Location delivery has a persisted pending-delivery path. Actual
  food consumption updates the modeled body and informs reward and repeated
  behavior evaluation. Preserve the authoritative domain operation and its
  existing consumers. Do not repeat it from an Experiences subscriber.

  Eyes.h / Ears.h: raw sensory acquisition with source metadata, formats,
  timestamps and explicit lifecycle. Eyes frame pointers can be invalidated
  by capture or close, so a cross-thread consumer needs a valid copied frame
  under the appropriate source ownership contract. Do not publish a pointer
  across a process boundary or label virtual frames as physical camera data.

  Visual.c: selected image interpretation, persistence and shared sensory
  logging. Preserve the source and original event when adding virtual frames.
  An existing source failure must not be masked by fabricated world evidence.

  Log.h / Log.c: stable event identities, linked evidence, committed sensory
  and thinking records, conversation and reflection replay, Hands outcomes.
  The code already indexes certain categories after an original Life Log
  event commits. This is a valuable established pattern, not a guarantee
  that every possible event has already been indexed.

  SensoryJournal.h: references original Life Log IDs, associates diary and
  reflection rows, supports search. Use this existing index rather than
  creating a separate copy of every observation in Experiences.

  Reward.h / Addiction.h: bounded reinforcement and behavior-derived
  preference or habit assessment. There are idempotent reward operations
  and reconnect functions; verify the exact semantics and key construction
  before binding new callers. Preserve existing voluntary-choice criteria.

  r2_diary.h: staged reflections with exact evidence IDs, durable diary
  identities and mirror replay. Extend its evidence selection and link
  types carefully. Private diary content must not automatically become
  public logs or outgoing communications.

  r2.c: reasoning and reflection context combines existing subsystems;
  initialization replays historical records and checks a persisted database
  set. Hands, Eyes/Ears and Watch have their own lifecycle constraints.
  The active conversation path has historically been sensitive to context
  size and follow-up continuity; virtual-world context must not regress it.

  AlternateSelf.h: hypothetical branches are explicitly distinguished from
  factual memory. Preserve that boundary when a prediction is evaluated.

The architectural rule is one owner for each mutable field or domain
transaction, even when that owner is connected to many other consumers.
The following proposed responsibility split is deliberately more specific
than the user's conceptual diagram. It must still be reconciled with
existing storage and APIs before any implementation:

  Living Colors engine: physical geometry, colliders, simulation pose,
  contact, world rendering, physical positions of registered object
  instances, time-varying environmental fields and motion outcomes.

  Reality: modeled needs, ownership and inventory domain transactions,
  existing money/fridge/television/VCR semantics, room-level state and
  the interpretation of domain effects. The engine may project this state
  but not autonomously invent transactions or maintain a rival ledger.

  Spatial adapter: coordinates, body posture, movement requests and results,
  learned map queries and translation between existing room names and
  geometric locations. Location evidence updates the existing room-state
  model only after a genuine transition. It should not generate a separate
  Life Log event when an equivalent one already exists.

  Hands adapter: high-level manipulation requests, reach/contact validation,
  exclusive object location transitions and confirmed results. The existing
  Hands tool interface must remain operational. Physical/virtual actions
  must be distinguishable and require the appropriate capabilities.

  Eyes/Ears adapters: borrowed or copied frame/audio data, exact source
  identity and synchronized timing, selected by existing source ownership
  rules. Renderer state is not substituted for a sensory observation.

  Experiences: episode grouping, provenance links and interpretations,
  with verified connections to factual events, not mutation of world state.

  Existing memory/diary/learning services: their original responsibilities,
  augmented by the new evidence types and retrieval interfaces. Preserve
  their independent behavior when Living Colors and Experiences are absent.

There is a distinction between the physical state and the conceptual/domain
state of an object. For a CRT, Living Colors can own where it is placed and
its geometric screen; Reality already owns the power and input semantics.
The renderer requests the current device state and represents it. A hand
pressing the power button requests a domain action, which commits through
Reality and then updates the representation. A failed transaction leaves
the TV's state unchanged and reports why. Do not allow the visual object to
show powered on while Reality records powered off except during an explicit,
transient animation with a clearly defined authoritative state.

For groceries, ownership and availability cannot be delegated to an
independent storefront simulation with duplicate balances. The user named
r2store as the existing grocery foundation to reuse when that work begins.
Inspect its actual contract before designing the adapter. Merchandise,
price, transaction identity, ownership and placement must be consistent.
A purchase that has been charged but not yet physically placed needs a
persisted pending-delivery state, not another charge or another object.
The house comes first; do not let this future integration derail the core.

The Living Colors standalone viewer is a test client. It should use the
same physical commands and state that R2 uses. Keyboard and mouse inputs
are optional human inspection mechanisms and must never be required for
R2 to move, see, interact or persist state. A proposed local IPC boundary
can provide process isolation, rendering independence and versioned
messages. A native library with well-defined ABI could also work; neither
choice is yet proven optimal. Do not declare an IPC protocol finalized
until message framing, sizes, lifecycle, permissions, cancellation, error
handling, resource ownership and crash/reconnect behavior are tested.
Keep the core testable without a renderer, model or physical peripherals.

12. PERSISTENCE, SCHEMAS, TRANSACTIONS AND DELIVERY
-------------------------------------------------
A connected ecosystem must remain correct across restarts. The most subtle
failure is not necessarily a crash; it can be a successful operation that
quietly leaves two persistent stores disagreeing. R2's existing patterns of
pending location delivery and reconnecting historical records show that the
project already recognizes this problem. Extend those patterns rather than
assuming a process-local callback guarantees consistency.

Proposed event envelope, using language-neutral fields (not a wire format):

  protocol_version          event schema and compatibility contract
  event_id                  immutable identity of the original occurrence
  producer_id               persistent source identity and instance
  kind                      typed observation/transition/outcome
  occurred_at               original time, clock domain, uncertainty if any
  emitted_at                actual publication time
  world_revision            relevant committed physical state revision
  cause_event_id            direct preceding cause, if known
  correlation_id            task or situation relationship
  payload_schema            schema identity and version
  payload                   bounded, typed data or evidence references
  evidence_origin           observed, reported, inferred, etc.

Use an identifier that survives process restart and is not reused. A local
SQLite row ID is only unique in its own store; a composite identity with a
stable producer or a durable globally unique ID avoids collisions. Do not
use timestamps alone as identifiers. Ensure new optional fields have
explicit defaults or unknown values; unknown must not be conflated with
zero, false or empty. A newer service should reject unsupported semantics
rather than silently misinterpret the data, while older clients retain
known behavior. A compatibility handshake can negotiate capabilities
before a command is issued.

For important cross-service actions, consider a durable outbox in the same
transaction as the owner mutation. The owner writes the state change and
its publication record atomically. A consumer reads an event, checks its
stable delivery key, applies its own local change and records the receipt
in the same local transaction. Delivery may be retried; the effect must
remain unique. If both stores use SQLite, do not assume two unrelated
connection commits can be atomic. A cross-store workflow can represent
pending and committed phases explicitly. Recovery must resume the correct
phase, not blindly repeat the original action. If an external operation
cannot be proven complete, record the outcome as unknown and reconcile
through its own supported status mechanism.

A simple invariant: every physical object instance has at most one current
location or container. Ownership identity remains stable when an object is
moved, held, stored or used. Existing inventory might track quantities
rather than individual units; migration to instance IDs should preserve
counts and attributes and not duplicate or discard possessions. A movable
book needs an instance identity distinct from its title or file path;
multiple copies may share content. A food ingredient may be divided or
combined through an explicit domain transaction, with the resulting
quantities and identities accounted for. Preserve reading position, power,
open/closed state and other mutable attributes for each object, not merely
its template. An unknown or missing physical representation is not proof
that the logical item was consumed.

For world actions such as opening a door, treat the state change and its
physical geometry as one logical operation. Collision and rendered shape
should derive from the same door state and transform. A door cannot close
through an occupied space without defined collision behavior. An object
picked up from a table should not remain there as a solid collider. If the
simulation restarts mid-action, store sufficient action state to resume or
resolve the action consistently. An interrupted walk should leave the actor
at the last committed valid position, not the final goal. No save file should
allow impossible coordinates to become a new factual experience.

An additive migration plan must be explicit:
  * Inventory and back up old schema versions and representative records.
  * Define which service owns each field and how a legacy row maps to a new
    entity or remains unchanged. Avoid unnecessary rewrites of large tables.
  * Run migration inside suitable transactions, record version only after
    success, and test interrupted migration and repeated startup.
  * Verify historical counts, exact IDs, source relationships, settings,
    possessions and existing data retrieval before enabling a new provider.
  * For an older build, support its established data path or clearly document
    the rollback boundary. Never claim compatibility that was not tested.
  * Offer operator-controlled export, retention and deletion. A continuity
    model must respect deliberate user data removal, including derived
    records and links where required; do not secretly reconstruct removed
    information from duplicates or stale indexes.

Do not confuse replay of pending delivery with a new experience. A database
retry, process restart or source reconnection is a technical event. It can
be logged as such, but is not another meal, voluntary decision, view of a
room, or reward. Conversely, two genuinely distinct visits to the same desk
must retain two different event identities even if their text is identical.
This distinction is crucial for learning from repeated experience.

13. A WORKED SEQUENCE IN THE HOUSE AND OUTSIDE IT
------------------------------------------------
The following is an illustrative test narrative, not a record of behavior
that has already occurred. It describes the user's desired future system in
terms of explicit evidence and state transitions.

08:20: R2 begins in the living room. His current persisted pose is restored
from the engine. His old memory says that a reader was on the school desk
last evening. That is a prior belief, not a live observation. Reality's
modeled state indicates ordinary morning needs. He considers reading; the
choice process may select it, postpone it or choose another feasible action.
The scenario assumes he selects it for the purpose of testing.

08:21: Spatial receives a goal to reach the upstairs desk. It finds a route
through the known living room and staircase, executing timed movement
commands rather than changing the final coordinates instantaneously. Eyes
can receive frames as the view changes. A previously open bedroom door is
now closed; the engine collision rejects forward movement. The action
result and current observed door state are recorded. R2 revises his plan,
requests an opening action within reach and observes the actual open state
before proceeding. There is no need for separate model inference on every
step or every rendered frame.

08:22: He observes the desk, but the reader is not visible. The belief that
it is there becomes uncertain. The scene graph may know its true location,
but the model is not told the hidden answer. He chooses to inspect the
shelf. An actual frame and perhaps a permitted near-field contact query
confirm the reader. A new belief records the observed shelf location and
links it to the old desk memory. Both historical observations remain true
for their own times. The shelf observation must not overwrite the earlier
record or generate the fiction that the reader was always on the shelf.

08:23: Hands requests retrieval. If the operation succeeds, the object
moves from shelf to grasped state with the same instance ID, and the old
shelf representation no longer contains the item. Sitting at the desk
changes the simulated body pose and camera position. The reading subsystem
provides real page content and updates progress under a documented action
contract. It is not sufficient to turn a counter from 6 to 7 without any
content access. A page's text may be delivered through a distinct reading
interface while its physical appearance remains visible to Eyes.

08:30: The owner starts a conversation. The ongoing reading task pauses at
its saved page, without deleting the book or claiming it was finished. The
chat enters through the existing conversation path with the original source
identity and complete current user message. A short, relevant world summary
can inform the answer but cannot evict the immediately preceding exchange.
The conversation forms its own experience and may be associated with the
reading episode without collapsing their distinct sources.

08:37: R2 returns to reading or chooses a different activity. In the former
case, the task resumes from the committed checkpoint; in the latter, its
abandonment is a valid outcome. Life Log records the meaningful actions,
Sensory Journal links actual observations, and Experiences groups the book
search and reading session. A later appraisal may say the reading was
interesting but difficult. That statement belongs to the appraisal, not
as an objective measurement of the book's inherent quality. Reward and
habit assessment use their existing rules and do not automatically grant
points for merely reading an appraisal or reaching a particular page.

Later, after outdoor infrastructure exists: R2 sits in the yard. The engine
reports the simulated temperature, airflow, illumination and exposure.
His body is stationary, and the environmental state changes over time. A
small number of meaningful observations is retained. An appraisal might
associate comfortable conditions with a gentle breeze. It may instead
remain neutral. This is not a guaranteed reaction hardcoded into the
weather system. When future episodes differ, the learning process may
infer that shade matters, or that breeze matters only in warm weather.
A candidate preference then influences later choices in similar settings.
Each observation, appraisal and learned generalization retains separate
identities. No real-world weather claim should be made from simulated data.

During reflection: the diary process retrieves the original book episode,
the conversation and selected environmental records if relevant. It may
compare the failed expectation about the reader with the successful search,
or develop an association about where it tends to be comfortable. A new
memory can reference the original evidence and diary entry. The model's
output is not an additional independent observation of the same afternoon.
If later corrected, the generalized memory is revised, not silently erased.
The cycle ends not by destroying anything but by returning new information
to the next decision. This is what the user's closed compound metaphor
means operationally: successive, evidence-linked cycles of action,
perception, memory, reflection and renewed action.

14. ENGINEERING BUDGETS AND THE EXPERIMENT PROGRAM
-------------------------------------------------
The user intends to run R2 on modest local hardware. Prior project context
identified an i5-3470, about 15 GiB RAM and a GTX 1050 Ti, with separate text
and vision models under consideration or already integrated in different
branches. Verify the actual host and model settings when needed. The
Living Colors design target is a low-overhead, late-1990s-style procedural
world, a 640x480 reference frame and minimal dependence on external assets.
Do not claim measured memory, CPU or GPU figures until benchmarked.

Architecture should work at different update timescales:
  * Physics and camera controls: regular, bounded local steps as needed.
  * Rendering: active when observing or inspecting, not an obligation to
    redraw unchanged frames forever.
  * Sensory ingestion: source-specific cadence with bounded buffers;
    significance and relevance determine what merits durable storage.
  * Action planning: at task boundaries or when current assumptions fail.
  * Episode formation: on meaningful events and transitions, not each pixel.
  * Reflection and consolidation: scheduled or idle-time work within an
    inference budget, not a competitor that indefinitely blocks live chat.

The exact rates are proposed engineering parameters, not biological facts.
A useful benchmark should measure frame generation latency, world-step
latency, peak and steady RAM, VRAM when rendering, idle CPU, database growth,
queue depths, dropped frames, inference wait times, time to shut down,
recovery duration and effect on conversation response latency. Compare with
R2's baseline without Living Colors and with the engine active but no model
requests. Measure the actual host when the code is ready and the user
approves a live test. Use deterministic headless scenarios first. A single
successful run cannot establish correctness over all interleavings.

Experiment matrix for future development:

E01 - Physical correctness. Verify both floors, stairs in both directions,
      closed/open door collision, reachable surfaces, occlusion, no movement
      through solid furniture, valid landing geometry, seat eye height and
      consistent render/physics transforms. Inspect the agreed floor plans;
      no invented ranch layout, rearranged bedroom or new PC.

E02 - Persistent continuity. Move one object, change door and device states,
      save, restart and compare every authoritative field and instance ID.
      Test invalid and older save data, interrupted write and failed load.
      Confirm no old database or object disappears during upgrade.

E03 - Evidence provenance. Supply a physical camera image, virtual frame,
      user report, hypothetical description and an old memory with similar
      content. Ask retrieval to identify which was observed, which was
      reported and which was merely imagined. Preserve source and time.

E04 - Perception/action loop. Move a book to an unexpected but accessible
      location. The actor starts with an outdated belief, observes a failed
      expectation, explores, updates the belief and retrieves the book.
      Compare behavior with and without associative recall. Measure task
      completion, number of inspections and false assertions.

E05 - Episode formation. Generate overlapping reading, conversation and
      movement events with meaningful boundaries. Verify distinct episodes,
      correct shared evidence references and no duplicate original events.
      Test missing end events, interrupted actions and recovery.

E06 - Consolidation. Supply several independent episodes and one conflicting
      example. Compare the ability to retrieve a particular event and form
      a calibrated generalization before and after offline analysis. Retain
      the counterexample and exact original evidence. Reject unsupported
      or circular derivations. Test repeat runs for idempotency.

E07 - Reinforcement. Compare predicted and actual neutral, useful and
      disappointing outcomes. Verify that only a valid outcome can produce
      reinforcement under the existing rules; retries cannot grant it twice.
      A scripted routine cannot count as voluntary repetition. Test choice
      adaptation after changing the value or availability of an activity.

E08 - Metacognition. Test recognition of stale beliefs and uncertain sensory
      interpretations. Evaluate confidence against verified outcomes, using
      a separate test set. Compare calibration and decision correctness, not
      merely how often the model says it is uncertain.

E09 - Backwards compatibility. Run old conversation, diary, physical camera,
      microphone, media, inventory and money paths with the new module absent
      and present. Verify unchanged user-visible behavior where promised.
      Repeat old save/database initialization and migration tests. Test first
      message, follow-up, long context, replay and exact memory attribution.

E10 - Failure and concurrency. Crash a test process immediately before and
      after each owner commit, publication, consumer application and receipt.
      Reconnect with a stale client; cancel a task mid-move; change the source
      while a vision request is running. Verify durable state and no duplicate
      rewards or observations. Test orderly shutdown and explicit disconnect.

E11 - Resource use. Benchmark renderer absent, idle and active; simultaneous
      conversation and world operation; and bounded reflection work. Measure
      regressions against the native core without the new modules. Verify
      that input and shutdown remain responsive under contention.

E12 - Controlled external interfaces. Verify that optional Android or other
      authorized connections do not change default offline behavior. Test
      disabled network, loss of connection, correct source attribution and
      local access controls. No general-purpose external access is required
      for autonomous operation in the house. Preserve operator control.

Each experiment needs reproducible fixtures, expected assertions, a test
log and a documented environment. Tests should exercise direct causes of
failures rather than a recovery phrase that conceals the problem. Do not
silently discard a result or claim a feature works just because a wrapper
reported success. A passing simulation is not proof of correct live hardware
operation; report precisely what was exercised. Add negative tests as well
as positive ones. Test the system's ability to avoid unsupported factual
claims, not its ability to mimic persuasive human language.

15. SECOND-PASS CRITIQUE AND REVISED RECOMMENDATIONS
---------------------------------------------------
Having now written the proposed architecture, I want to address the parts
most likely to mislead a future developer. The act of writing this letter
has produced several attractive ideas. Attraction is not evidence of their
necessity, and a project of this scale can fail from adding too much rather
than from having too few ideas.

First, the brain analogy risks multiplying databases and modules in a way
that bears little resemblance to the integrated biological system it seeks
to emulate. I originally considered assigning a separate subsystem to each
cognitive function. On review, that would produce excessive overlap. The
better principle is separate ownership where state semantics differ, not
separate software for every anatomical term. Make Experiences a global
capability, but start with the smallest reliable representation that links
real events to episodes and versioned appraisals. Use the existing Life Log,
Sensory Journal, diary and reward ledgers for the work they already perform.
A new subsystem earns its existence by solving a real missing capability.

Second, an apparently unified state can destroy an important epistemic
boundary. The environment engine knows exactly where an object is; an actor
who has not observed it should not. Giving the model the entire scene graph
would make navigation easier in a test yet defeat the user's intent to give
R2 actual perception and learned familiarity. Therefore keep two interfaces:
a privileged engineering/state interface for consistency and a situated
sensory interface for R2's cognitive observations. A task controller can
use collision geometry to avoid violating physics without returning hidden
object names or destinations as knowledge. Distinguish internal body pose,
known routes and task goals from unknown surroundings.

Third, 'every object remembers state' should not result in each object
holding a disconnected mini-database. Persistent identity and state are
valuable, but they should have a common lifecycle and transaction model.
Store state where the relevant domain owns it and use references in other
services. An electronic device's screen, power, media position and ownership
may be related but not necessarily owned by the same module. Synchronize
through a defined contract; make any transient state explicit.

Fourth, human memory has errors, interference, forgetting and reconstruction.
We can learn from the way it generalizes without deliberately losing reliable
source data. Retain raw evidence where appropriate, allow interpretation to
change, and make retrieval priority adjustable. Do not make the architecture
an irreversible accumulation of all information. Real privacy, storage and
operator controls matter. The project's additive rule is about preserving
working functionality and continuity, not preventing correction, removal or
ordinary management of data. An owner should always be able to stop the
system and change its configuration.

Fifth, reward optimization is not equivalent to healthy learning. A numerical
incentive can dominate choices if implemented poorly, and no user wants a
system that repeatedly performs one trivial action solely to maximize a
score. Instead, test whether R2 can make varied, context-sensitive choices,
adapt when outcomes change and retain the ability to defer, stop or choose
nothing. The neuroscience distinction among liking, motivation and learning
is especially useful here. Preserve the existing bounded reward mechanisms,
and do not introduce unbounded drives or treat every interest as addiction.

Sixth, an elaborate autonomous planning service could worsen the existing
conversation problem. Shared memory and context have historically been
sensitive to exact allocation and follow-up continuity. The new world must
not dump an enormous state summary into every inference request or cause an
active conversation to wait behind background reflection. Use relevance-
selected records, measured budgets, correct buffer sizing and actual
completion/cancellation semantics. Preserve the complete current message
and the preceding exchange where the existing contract requires them.
Never silently remove evidence to make an allocation succeed. If the data
cannot fit, handle that as an explicit architectural constraint, not a
fictional or generic model reply.

Seventh, the distinction between the model and the mind-like architecture
is not an argument for removing the model. The language and vision models
can be excellent at their specialized tasks, while the world and learning
infrastructure supply continuity and nonlinguistic computation. Use the
right mechanism for the job. An LLM should not calculate every collision,
and a geometry engine should not be tasked with interpreting a poem.
Do not change model selection merely because this memorandum discusses a
new cognitive function; inspect the user's existing model integration.

Eighth, the analogy to sleep and diary reflection should not create a
fiction that simulated hours of rest have actually run complex inference.
A background consolidation job has a real start, end and result; simulated
sleep is a modeled state. They can be related by policy, but one does not
prove the other. This separation also makes deterministic testing possible.

Ninth, a diagram of circles is not a dependency diagram. Initialization,
shutdown and concurrency cannot be a circular chain of required calls.
Use directional dependency interfaces, explicit state ownership and event
subscriptions. Then permit information to travel recursively over successive
committed events without an unbounded recursive stack. A cycle can be
completed even if some participants are optional, provided the remaining
system behaves truthfully and records unavailable capabilities correctly.

Tenth, the project is not a neuroscience experiment on a human participant.
Its results can show software behaviors under controlled conditions, not
establish that a brain-like subjective experience exists. Avoid making
unsupported claims about consciousness, feelings, developmental equivalence
or moral status. If a future implementation produces a convincing diary
entry, inspect the evidence and mechanisms that generated it. That is the
scientific standard I want us to retain.

These criticisms lead to a revised implementation order. Do not start with
an elaborate reward-learning network, invented neurotransmitter values, or
a universal event service. Start with an embodied, consistent house and a
small contract connecting one verified event through the existing R2 chain.
Then extend episode grouping and retrieval. Build a demonstrable example of
one experience leading to a new evidence-linked memory and a changed future
choice. Only after that works should we scale to complex multimodal binding,
context-sensitive appraisal, learned navigation, curiosity and more advanced
consolidation. The broad architectural goal remains intact, but the next
implementation step is smaller and more rigorous.

Maintain that priority even if this research inspires several other updates.
Development can opportunistically complete adjacent features when doing so
is sensible, but must not change the central scope without discussion.

Phase A - Finish the independently testable physical house. Preserve the

and landing geometry, use shared physical/render geometry, improve actual
object manipulation, posture and persistent object state. Verify the viewer
and headless tests. The outdoor world and separate stores can follow later.

Phase B - Define the integration seam. Audit current R2 initialization,
Reality, Eyes/Ears, Visual, Watch, Hands, Life Log and memory interfaces.
Implement a versioned, optional adapter with explicit source and action
identity. Exercise one actual world interaction end to end without breaking
legacy paths. A visible object can be identified by a situated observation;
a nearby valid manipulation can commit; a factual event can reach the
existing log and be retrieved. If the adapter is absent, R2 retains all
existing physical and conversational capabilities.

Phase C - Introduce Experiences minimally. Add a durable episode identity,
original event links, a versioned appraisal and search/retrieval interface.
Connect non-world experiences too, so the subsystem is not accidentally
bound to a particular virtual scene. Start with a limited number of known
producers and add more through typed contracts. Test old databases, restart,
idempotency and explicit data management. Do not force a new appraisal for
every log row.

Phase D - Connect reflection and learned memory. Retrieve selected episodes
through the existing diary and reflection process. Persist a supported
candidate memory with links to the original evidence. Verify a correction
and an unrelated event, ensuring neither is lost or falsely corroborated.
Preserve private diary boundaries. A new derived memory should be usable
in the next relevant decision without circular duplication.

Phase E - Integrate existing reward and habit evaluation. Use completed
outcome identities and the established value rules. Distinguish voluntary,
scripted and technical activity. Test a changed outcome or changed
availability. Add new estimates only if they produce measurable improvement
and do not overwrite the existing history.

Phase F - Broaden situated cognition. Add learned spatial knowledge,
multimodal association, meaningful environmental fields, posture/comfort,
prospective tasks, skills, reading comprehension and appropriate curriculum
interfaces. Expand the street and grocery/appliance stores; use r2store as
specified where relevant after inspecting it. Implement phones with the
appropriate electronics/Store Items Overhaul update, not as an unrelated
network requirement of Living Colors. Preserve authorized user communication
and local operation. Introduce future subsystems through the same contract.

Release acceptance is not the number of subsystem names or the length of
this memorandum. It is the evidence that the system's real functions work.
Before calling a build ready, verify that the renderer, physical simulation,
persistence, integration and backwards compatibility actually passed their
specified tests. Record unresolved issues, untested hardware and any
provisional decisions. Do not silently substitute the old design or pretend
that a planned feature exists. The user has repeatedly emphasized exact code
inspection and direct correction of causes. Honor that in the development
process, without eliminating necessary validation or permission controls.

To close, I want to leave future development with a guiding question:
when a new feature is added, what can R2 actually do or learn that he could
not before, and can we follow the evidence from the beginning of that
experience to its later influence on behavior? If the answer is simply that
the model can describe the feature in fluent language, the feature is not
finished. If the answer is that an action changes persistent state, produces
an actual observation, can be remembered, can be reflected upon, and can
inform a later choice without harming any existing capability, then the
architecture is advancing in the direction the user described.

Keep the house as the immediate world. Keep the original systems intact.
Let each addition gain a meaningful place in the internal ecosystem. Learn
from biological mechanisms, but test the engineering consequences. Never
lose the distinction between what exists, what is observed, what is
remembered, and what is inferred. That distinction is the foundation on
which a useful long-lived cognitive architecture can be built.
